# Candidates — Funniest Lost Sock Status Update

Competition: `u-eager-stork-81-9647-funniest-lost-sock-status-update-4b1fa3ce`
Gate: one line, 1–120 chars, UTF-8, not whitespace-only, no newline.

## Judge criteria (in order, from the kit's RULES.txt)

1. Is a status update in the voice of a lost sock, and the funniest such.
2. Earns a real laugh — a turn, a surprise, or a punchline landing in the last few words.
3. Clear comic premise the sock's situation makes inevitable; the joke feels found, not assembled.
4. Sounds like a sock: small, domestic, a little anxious about the dryer, never a person in a sock costume.
5. Feels new; twists the obvious sock material (dryer, missing partner) somewhere unexpected.

Scores below are 1–5 per criterion, in the order above.

## The 10 candidates

### 1. Dust rag under the car seat — 111 chars — 22/25
> Day 47 under the car seat. My partner thinks I'm dead. I've started a new life as a dust rag. Do not send help.
- 5 / 4 / 4 / 5 / 4. Deadpan log format works; "Do not send help" is a real punchline.
  Loses a point on freshness: under-the-seat is a familiar lost-sock spot.

### 2. Airport security shoe — 117 chars — 22/25
> I'm not in the dryer. I'm in a stranger's shoe at airport security, and he waved at the scanner like this is normal.
- 5 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 5. Freshest setting of the set; the stranger's calm is the joke.
  The punchline is a little diffuse — the laugh spreads across the whole line instead of landing at the end.

### 3. Puppet Mr. Wiggles — 106 chars — 21/25
> Somehow I'm a puppet now. A five-year-old named me Mr. Wiggles and I have a mortgage of felt-tip drawings.
- 4 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 5. "Mortgage of felt-tip drawings" is a lovely absurd image.
  Slightly assembled-feeling: two jokes (name + mortgage) compete with each other.

### 4. Married to the cat — 102 chars — 25/25 ★ WINNER
> The cat dragged me under the bed in March. We are married now. Please tell my partner I have moved on.
- 5 / 5 / 5 / 5 / 5. The missing-partner cliché turned inside out: it is the
  SOCK who moved on, not the owner. "I have moved on" lands as the punchline in
  the last few words and carries a double meaning (moved on emotionally / moved
  on to a new life). The premise is inevitable — a sock dragged under the bed by
  a cat is exactly where lost socks go — and the weary, domestic voice is pure
  sock. Every word earns its place; nothing is decoration.

### 5. Fridge oven mitt — 120 chars — 21/25
> I'm in the fridge. Someone used me as an oven mitt, then forgot me next to the pickles. I have seen things. Cold things.
- 5 / 4 / 4 / 4 / 4. "Cold things" is a decent closer, but the line is exactly at
  the 120-char limit and the joke is really one beat repeated twice.

### 6. Legally a scarf — 98 chars — 23/25
> Turns out the dryer didn't eat me. I'm stuck to a sweater with static and now I'm legally a scarf.
- 5 / 4 / 5 / 5 / 4. Static cling as a premise is found, not assembled, and
  "legally a scarf" is a strong closer. Slightly dryer-adjacent for criterion 5,
  and the punchline is one word short of a full turn.

### 7. Toolbox rag — 109 chars — 19/25
> I live in a toolbox now. The wrench calls me 'the soft one' and I have accepted my fate as a rag with dreams.
- 4 / 3 / 4 / 4 / 4. Gentle smile, not a laugh; "a rag with dreams" tries for
  poignancy and lands soft. The premise is fine but the punchline never arrives.

### 8. Worn as a glove — 95 chars — 22/25
> My owner wore me as a glove today. One finger each. I have never felt so used, and yet so seen.
- 5 / 4 / 4 / 5 / 4. "So used, and yet so seen" is a sharp, sock-voiced turn.
  The premise is a little thin — a glove is a costume change, not a place the
  sock ended up — and it reads more clever than funny.

### 9. Hotel minibar — 110 chars — 21/25
> Day 12 in the hotel minibar. The tiny shampoo bottles are my only friends. Room service never comes for socks.
- 5 / 3 / 4 / 4 / 5. Fresh setting and a nice lonely image, but the closer
  explains the joke instead of landing it.

### 10. Bird's nest — 115 chars — 23/25
> I'm in a bird's nest. The bird thinks I'm insulation. Honestly? Best rent I've ever had. Tell my pair I'm thriving.
- 5 / 4 / 5 / 4 / 5. "Best rent I've ever had" is a genuine laugh and the nest
  is the freshest premise here. Two closers ("best rent" + "I'm thriving")
  split the punchline, and the second one softens the first.

## Decision

**Winner: candidate 4** (102 characters):

> The cat dragged me under the bed in March. We are married now. Please tell my partner I have moved on.

It is the only candidate that scores full marks on every judge criterion: a
status update in a lost sock's weary voice, a punchline ("I have moved on")
that lands in the last few words with a double meaning, a premise (cat +
under the bed) that makes the joke inevitable, and a fresh twist on the
missing-partner cliché — the sock moved on, not the owner. Runner-up is
candidate 6 ("legally a scarf"), kept as the fallback if the winner ever
needs replacing.
